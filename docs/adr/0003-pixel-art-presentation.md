# Pixel-art presentation: golden-hour palette, nearest sampling, baked world canvas

The v1 look is pixel art at a fixed 8 texels per world unit: global nearest sampling (`ImagePlugin::default_nearest` on the `DefaultPlugins` install, the engine's documented pixel-art configuration) with integer texel-to-screen mapping inside the letterboxed 16:9 viewport (~80x45 world units visible), one project palette (golden-hour remap of Endesga 32, authored in `assets/palette/golden-hour.gpl`, to which all future art quantizes), and a baked world canvas (the static world rasterized once at load into a single pixel canvas, with only Cars and FX drawn per frame).

Nearest-plus-integer-mapping keeps texels crisp at any window size instead of smearing them through linear filtering, and the single raised-contrast palette keeps the track readable at speed: olive-to-golden grass against a near-black charcoal asphalt ramp, saturated kerb red, foliage darker and cooler than grass, one amber accent. Baking the static world into one canvas buys the look without a per-frame tile bill, and the all-original-art posture (CC0 donor FX only) keeps licensing trivial. The cost is pipeline discipline: every sprite must quantize to the 32 project colors, world visuals are immutable at runtime once baked, and camera zoom can only move in integer texel steps.

Considered and rejected: keeping the flat-vector quads (fails the look the spec asks for); textured top-down with photographic textures (needs a binary-heavy art pipeline for no readability gain); adopting free pixel-art car packs — MinZinn's Pixel Vehicles (CC-BY 4.0 plus 8-directional sheets) and marcusvh's 2D cars (credit asked) are both excluded under the CC0-only ceiling, usable only as eyeball proportion reference for the original Car Sprites. Research basis: `docs/research/pixel-art-racing-assets.md`; spec issue #32.
