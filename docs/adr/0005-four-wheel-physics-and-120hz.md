# Four-wheel handling at 120 Hz, with per-wheel presentation

Issue #62 replaces the point-mass grip damper with a planar four-wheel vehicle in a private core module: load transfer, load-sensitive Pacejka-style tire curves with a combined-force ceiling, a rear-wheel drivetrain with a bounded limited-slip coupling, and a four-speed automatic transmission. The old damping model and its canned trajectory tests are removed at cutover. This supersedes ADR-0001's point-mass choice while retaining Bevy, custom physics, and the engine-free simulation boundary.

The user explicitly chose 120 Hz after considering 60, 64, and 120 Hz. Each public tick uses four tire-force substeps (480 Hz). Higher control/rule cadence costs more CPU; substeps stabilize the wheel dynamics without exposing another simulation boundary. Tick-based timers retain their wall-time meanings: three-second countdown and Recovery cooldown, one-second resume preparation, quarter-second reverse engagement, six-second pass commitment, and 45-second finish window. AI reverse recovery is now two seconds to clear a wall under the new acceleration envelope. Old 64 Hz input streams and empirical lap-time pins are not replay-compatible with this handling version.

Drift is telemetry for rear lateral saturation with countersteering, never an effects gate. Each tire independently supplies its contact offset, surface, normalized longitudinal/lateral slip, and grip-limit flag. Marks follow those contact patches; screech follows continuous tire excess, including front lockup and rear wheelspin. A locked tire uses kinetic friction opposing contact-patch travel. Ordinary gripped cornering is silent and leaves no marks.

Stability assist supplies only a bounded dissipative yaw moment when rear slip exceeds front slip beyond peak grip. It cannot reshape steering demand or steering linkage. It defaults on for the player and off for AI Opponents. G selects it in the menu, alongside F's steering response; the selected policy is visible during Race and Corner Practice and survives restart. AI Opponents still return only CarInput, using front tire grip for speed planning, geometric pure pursuit, and axle-slip estimates from perceived motion. Finite steering response also requires reserving a rival corridor until seven units clear, starting before physical overlap; this extends ADR-0002’s 4.4-unit overlap protection without changing the collision box. Recovery and clearance rules remain in core.

This amends ADR-0004: Race and Corner Practice keys advance to handling2 and include stability-assist identity. The existing records-v2.json format is unchanged; old entries stay stored under their original keys. No old best is offered as a target under the new handling or a different assist policy. Changing policy during an active lap invalidates that lap and clears the simulation's cached eligible best.
